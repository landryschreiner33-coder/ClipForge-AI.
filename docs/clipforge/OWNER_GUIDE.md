# ClipForge: a short guide

## Signing in (cloud mode only)
Open your studio's address and choose **Continue with Google**. Only your own account is let in; anyone else sees
"This studio is private" and nothing else. **Sign out** is in the top bar; Settings can end every session at once.
On the desktop app there is no sign-in.

## Making clips
Home → **Make clips**. Pick **Highlights** (the strongest short moments), **Full story** (one complete clip per
story) or **Story parts** (each story cut at its best moments into Part 1, Part 2…), drop your video, and choose a
style (*My saved style* is used unless you pick another).
**Customize captions and framing** changes only this project. The box *Used from your Brain* shows which style, skill
or note decides each choice before you start.
- **Make clips only**: review and download, publish later.
- **Make & upload clips**: tick the exact channels. YouTube uploads happen once per channel after each clip passes
  its final check; TikTok posts wait for your OK on each one. You can close the page once the file has uploaded.

**How long clips are.** Settings → Defaults sets the Shortest clip, the Ideal clip length and the Longest clip (25,
45 and 90 seconds unless you change them). ClipForge finds the strongest lines of a moment and then takes in what
leads up to them and how they end, as long as it is still the same subject, so a clip is the whole moment and not
only its punchline. Telling ClipForge AI *remember: clips should be at least 40 seconds* changes the lengths for
new clips.

**Your Brain picks with you.** What you told ClipForge AI (*I love funny moments*, *never clip the sponsor read*),
the advice in your switched-on skills about picking clips, the viral lessons you imported and what your own
results show all nudge which moments are picked. Each clip's page says what your Brain added or took away and why.
Once enough of your posts have real numbers, ClipForge AI compares which kinds of moment did better and keeps it in
Brain → Memory as a line that starts *From your results:*; switch it off or forget it there if you disagree.

Full story finds where each story starts (the narrator's own words such as *next story*, *first story* or *story
number three*, a post's title read out, a long break and a new subject) and makes one clip per story, running until
the next story starts. A gamer who stops talking for a few seconds while he plays is not split at every pause. A
story is never cut, except one longer than **five minutes**: no story clip is ever longer than that, so a longer
story is split at its best point into Part 1, Part 2…

Story parts finds the stories the same way and cuts each one where it is most gripping to stop (a cliffhanger, a
reveal, a jump in time, the poster's update) into parts of about one to three minutes. Each part is its own clip with
the story's title and "Part 1", "Part 2"… at the top.

Story clips keep the picture still: the whole video, centred, never cropped, zoomed or stretched, with the captions
just above it so none of the gameplay is covered. A framing you choose under *Customize* for this video, or in the
clip editor, changes that (choosing a subject to follow there, such as a face, gives the vertical frame following
it). Every clip is made right away; nothing waits for you. Each card shows the start, the end,
the length, the part and how sure ClipForge is. If a boundary is off, use *Start 2 s earlier*, *End 2 s later*,
*Split*, *Merge* or *Leave out*, then *Remake this clip* (none of them can make a story clip longer than five
minutes). A story longer than an account allows is kept whole; the card says which accounts can take it. With
ClipForge AI's model set up, it double-checks the boundaries the rules are least sure of, names the stories and helps
choose where parts end.

## Making a YouTube video
Home → **Make a YouTube video**. Drop your clips and images, put them in order, answer the four questions and press
**Build my video**. You get a rough cut and a preview. Change it with *Move*, *Remove this clip*, *Shorten the
introduction*, captions, transitions and music level; every change is a new version you can go back to. **Render**
makes the full-quality file on the server; **Download** gives you that exact checked version.
**Remember this style** (beside the brief) saves your editing answers for next time.

**Big footage.** On the desktop, **Add from this computer** uses files where they are: nothing is copied or
uploaded, so a 17 GB recording is added in a moment (keep it where it is while the project exists). Long files are
listened to in pieces and a closed app carries on where it stopped; previews of big videos read a small copy, the
final render always your original.

**Your script.** Drop the script you read from (text, Word, PDF or Final Draft) or paste it. ClipForge finds every
line in your footage and cuts one clip per line in script order, using the take you said most cleanly; the other
takes stay behind each line, and **Use this take** in the Script panel swaps one in. Lines starting with # become
chapters. A script can also be added or changed later from the editor page.

**Edit it for me** now also keeps the cleanest take of a line you said again in another file, leaves out warm-ups
("is this on?", "take two") and words to whoever is recording ("did we get that?"), and opens a video of two minutes
or more with its strongest line (a cold open; Remove the first clip if you would rather not). With a model set up,
ClipForge AI reads the whole video, as long as its time budget allows.

## ClipForge AI: talk to it, and its memory
ClipForge AI is the assistant inside the editor. On a video, the **ClipForge AI** panel under the clips takes what you
want changed (*move the summit part before lunch*, *make the captions smaller*) and proposes a checked change: nothing
happens until you press **Apply as a new version** (Undo takes it back) or **Discard**. Teach it in any chat with
*remember: …*, *always …* or *never …* (add *for this video* to keep it to one video); ask *what do you know?*,
*why?* or *help*; say *forget …* to remove something. **Talk to AI** (in the menu right under Studio, as a Brain tab
and on the Studio's side panel) opens **Talk to ClipForge AI**, the chat for everything else. Ask it how to edit
(*how loud should my video be?*, *where do captions go on TikTok?*, *what music fits a sad scene?*, *what makes a
good title?*) or how ClipForge works (*how do I clip a live stream?*, *where do I change the clip length?*) and it
answers from its built-in know-how and its guide to the app, with no model, and links the
page; with a model set up (Settings → **ClipForge AI**;
the built-in one is free and stays on this computer) it answers anything, with your memories and that know-how.
Brain → **Memory** shows everything it remembers, where each memory was used, and lets you edit, confirm, turn off or
forget it, tidy or rebuild the brain, and export or import it as a file; below that, **What it knows about editing**
lists the sixteen playbooks it edits by (hooks, endings, pacing, captions, sound, music, colour, effects, picking
clips, platforms, retention, talking heads, animated shorts, titles, post text and hashtags, gaming and stream
clips). What you teach it always wins over them. Titles, post text and hashtags are still written only from the
words said in the clip; tell it how you like them (*remember: titles in lower case, no emoji*, *at most three
hashtags*, *don't ask people to follow*) and both the clip's post text and Autopilot's follow it. Only words are ever sent to a
model, never your videos or their sound.

## Brain: styles and skills
Brain → **Styles** lists your saved styles. Brain → **Skills** imports a Markdown or JSON skill: you see what it would
change, what it ignores and anything it refused (commands, secrets, accounts, publishing), then switch it on. Try
*Add the example skill*. Changing a style or skill affects new projects only. The advice in a switched-on skill
that is not an editing choice is read by ClipForge AI when it answers you, picks clips and writes titles.

## Accounts
**Accounts** → *Add YouTube account* or *Add TikTok account*. Add as many as you have; adding one never replaces
another. *Reconnect* fixes an expired sign-in for that account only; *Make default* is used when a post names no
account; *Disconnect* stops posts to that account and leaves the others alone.

## Robots and the Office
**Office** shows the robot team doing real work. **Office → Team**: press *Rename* under any robot, type a name (up
to 32 characters) and *Save*; *Reset name* brings the original back. A name never changes what a robot does.

## Look
**Settings → Appearance**: theme (follow system, light, dark), accent colour and spacing follow your account; motion
and the layout of each screen are kept for this device. *Reset layout* touches layouts only.
