Turn long videos into short clips, on your own computer

ClipFoundry is a desktop app for Windows and for Macs with Apple silicon. It finds the best moments in a long video and turns them into vertical clips with captions for YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Reels. Your videos stay on your computer. Only the clips you post are uploaded.

Get ClipFoundry on GitHub How it works

There is no ClipFoundry account and no ClipFoundry cloud service: the app runs on your computer and opens in your web browser (on a Mac, in its own window).

What it does

Finds the best moments

It writes down what is said in your video, on your computer (fast with an NVIDIA graphics card), and picks moments that start strong and end on a payoff.

Makes vertical clips

9:16 framing that follows faces or the person talking, with word-by-word captions in a few clean styles.

Writes titles from the clip

Titles, captions and hashtags are built from the words said in the clip, and a post can also carry a short call to action and a credit line. AI suggestions that add names or numbers not said in the clip are thrown out. These checks match words automatically and can miss things. Text you type yourself is not removed.

You decide what gets posted

When you add a video you choose Make clips only, which keeps its clips on your computer, or Make & upload clips. With Make & upload clips you confirm, one time, your basis for using that video, the YouTube account or accounts and the audience for its clips, and your made-for-kids answer. Its clips that pass the final checks then upload to YouTube without a separate approval for each. Every other post uploads, through the official APIs, only after you approve it or, for YouTube only, when you deliberately turn review mode off, under an automatic-publishing permission you turn on. There is no app daily cap by default, and eligible clips can be planned at any hour; optional limits and platform waits still apply. TikTok always needs your OK on each post. You explicitly choose Public or Private for YouTube; existing Private plans stay Private. TikTok offers only the audiences permitted for your account and app. Reuse permission and final-file checks apply to every automatic upload. You can also export clips and post them yourself.

Autopilot, if you want it

It makes clips from each video link or file you add to it, in the background. It does not search the internet for videos or watch your videos folder. You are responsible for having the right to use each video.

Real numbers only

Results show the views and likes YouTube and TikTok report. Scores are marked as estimates, and nothing is made up.

How it works

VIDEO → TRANSCRIPT → BEST MOMENTS → CLIPS → 9:16 → CAPTIONS → TITLES → CHECK → POST OR EXPORT

  1. Install it and start it. On Windows, download ClipFoundry, extract it and double-click Open-ClipFoundry.cmd (start.bat if you downloaded the source code); it opens in your browser. On a Mac, download and open the DMG, drag ClipFoundry into Applications and open it; it opens in its own window. The Mac app is an unsigned preview that Apple has not notarized, so the first time macOS may ask you to allow it under System Settings, Privacy & Security, Open Anyway. Either way it runs at 127.0.0.1, an address only your own computer can reach.
  2. Add a video. Choose a file, or paste the link to one public recorded video (not a live stream). Autopilot takes a link or a file the same way. It does not search for videos; it works only on the videos you add. Adding a link does not give you the right to reuse that video.
  3. Check the clips. Watch them, trim them or change the captions, then export them.
  4. Post, if you like. Connect YouTube or TikTok, then approve each post, or choose Make & upload clips when you add a video, so its clips that pass the final checks upload to YouTube by themselves. Before Autopilot posts a clip, ClipFoundry checks the exact final file. A clip you post yourself from its Prepare post page is uploaded when you confirm; if Autopilot made the clip or ClipFoundry already checked it, that file must pass the check first.

Connecting YouTube and TikTok

  • You sign in on Google's or TikTok's own page. ClipFoundry never sees or stores your password.
  • On YouTube it asks to manage your videos (it only uploads them), to see your YouTube account and to see YouTube Analytics reports. It uses them to upload your posts, to show your channel's name and picture, and to read the status and results of your posts. Autopilot no longer searches for videos. For a video that an earlier version of ClipFoundry found, and only if you entered no YouTube API key of your own, ClipFoundry also uses your YouTube sign-in to check which channel posted it.
  • On TikTok it asks to show your name and picture, to post videos or send them to your TikTok inbox, and to read the results of your posts.
  • Before a TikTok post you choose who can see it, whether people can comment, duet or stitch, and whether it is promotional, and you confirm TikTok's music rules. TikTok always needs your OK on each post.
  • The platforms' own limits apply. For example, until TikTok audits the app, posts sent straight to TikTok can only be seen by you. ClipFoundry tells you so instead of working around it, and can send the video to your TikTok inbox instead, where you finish and post it in the TikTok app.
  • You can disconnect an account at any time, in ClipFoundry or in your Google or TikTok settings.

Read exactly what is stored and sent in the Privacy Policy.

What you need

  • A Windows 10 or 11 PC (64-bit), with 8 GB of memory or more, or a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 15 or newer.
  • On Windows: Python 3.11 or 3.12, and FFmpeg. The setup guide shows how to install both. The Mac app includes them.
  • An NVIDIA graphics card is optional. Without one, transcription works, just more slowly.
  • About 3 GB of free disk space (about 6 GB with an NVIDIA graphics card), plus room for your videos.
  • To post from the app: your own YouTube channel and Google Cloud project, and your own TikTok developer app. Exporting clips needs no account at all.
