Privacy Policy

ClipFoundry · Effective October 2, 2026

The short version

  • ClipFoundry is a desktop app that runs on your own computer. Your videos, clips, transcripts, settings and account connections are stored there. We don't run any server that receives them.
  • We, the publisher, receive nothing from the app: no videos, no account details, no usage statistics and no crash reports. The app has no ads, no tracking and no usage analytics.
  • Information leaves your computer only for features that need another service, for example posting to YouTube or TikTok, Autopilot's searches for videos, downloading the speech-recognition model, or an optional AI service you turn on. It goes straight from your computer to that service.
  • You sign in on Google's and TikTok's own pages. ClipFoundry never sees or stores your passwords.
  • Deleting a video in ClipFoundry removes ClipFoundry's copy of it, its clips and its transcript from your computer. Some records stay, such as your post history (section 7). Deleting does not remove a video you already posted on YouTube or TikTok. You do that on the platform.

1. Who this policy is from

ClipFoundry is published by Landry Schreiner ("we", "us"). You can reach us at landryschreiner456@gmail.com. This policy covers the ClipFoundry desktop app and this website.

The app runs on your computer and under your control, so you decide what it processes. We have no access to the information stored in it.

2. What ClipFoundry stores on your computer

ClipFoundry keeps its information in files in its data folder (the data folder inside the ClipFoundry folder, unless you chose another one). When you start Autopilot it also creates your videos folder (Videos\ClipFoundry) for videos you want it to use.

ClipFoundry doesn't use cookies. The Reduce motion preference stays in your browser's local storage on this computer and is not sent to a platform. Clearing browser storage removes it; your operating system's motion preference still applies. This preference does not change video files or processing settings. When a screen shows your YouTube or TikTok profile picture, your browser loads that picture from Google's or TikTok's servers, which can see your computer's IP address.

4. YouTube API Services

ClipFoundry uses the YouTube API Services. By using its YouTube features you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google handles your information as the Google Privacy Policy describes.

When you connect YouTube, ClipFoundry asks Google for these permissions:

  • Manage your YouTube videos (youtube.upload). ClipFoundry uses it only to upload videos: the posts you approve, and the posts Autopilot publishes under the automatic-publishing permission you turned on.
  • View your YouTube account (youtube.readonly), for your channel's name and picture, your channel's newest uploads, and the status and statistics of videos posted with ClipFoundry. If Autopilot is on and you have not entered your own YouTube API key, Autopilot also uses this permission to find videos: it searches YouTube for your topics, reads YouTube's lists of popular and live videos, lists the new videos of your channel and of channels you added, gave a permission to or have an agreement with, reads the public details of the videos it finds, and checks which channel posted a video.
  • View YouTube Analytics reports (yt-analytics.readonly), for shares, watch time and how much of your posts people watched.

ClipFoundry uses this information on your computer: to publish, to show you status and results, to find videos for Autopilot, and to plan future posts. Some of it leaves your computer in two cases. If a clip comes from someone else's YouTube video under a Creative Commons license, its post on YouTube or TikTok includes a credit line with that video's title, channel name and link. If you turn on a cloud AI service, the YouTube title of a video Autopilot clips is sent to that service as the video's name (section 3). Nothing is sent to us.

ClipFoundry deletes YouTube information that is 30 days old: videos Autopilot found but did not use, the statistics of the videos it found, and the statistics of your own posts. It does this each time it starts and about once an hour while Autopilot is on. Your own posts' statistics are kept longer only if you turn on "Google approved derived metrics" in Settings because Google approved your project to keep them, and only while your channel stays connected. Some YouTube information is kept longer: your channel's name and picture link, until you disconnect; your channel's name in the saved wording of the automatic-publishing permission (if you ever turned it on), the title, channel and link of each video Autopilot worked on, and the ID and link of each of your posts, until you delete the data folder; and video titles in Autopilot's event history (90 days) and in the background log file (until you delete it).

You can remove ClipFoundry's access at any time in ClipFoundry (Settings → Accounts → Disconnect), or on Google's security settings page at https://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions. To delete what ClipFoundry stored on your computer, see section 7.

5. TikTok

When you connect TikTok, ClipFoundry asks TikTok for these permissions:

  • user.info.basic, for your display name and profile picture.
  • video.upload, to send a video to your TikTok inbox as a draft that you finish in the TikTok app.
  • video.publish, to post a video you approved straight to your profile (unless you turn this off).
  • video.list, for the statistics of your own videos (unless you turn this off).

ClipFoundry stores your sign-in tokens, your TikTok account ID, your display name, a link to your profile picture and the permissions you granted. For each post it stores the caption, the choices you made, the post's ID and status, and its statistics. For a post made straight to your profile, it also stores your TikTok user name. It reads the posting options TikTok offers for your account fresh each time, before you approve or post a video, and doesn't keep them. TikTok handles your information as TikTok's Privacy Policy describes. You can disconnect TikTok in ClipFoundry, or remove the app's access in the TikTok app's settings.

6. How your information is protected

  • On Windows, sign-in tokens, API keys and client secrets are encrypted with Windows' own data protection (DPAPI), so only your Windows account on that computer can read them. If Windows can't encrypt one, ClipFoundry stores it without encryption and notes that in its log. On other systems they are stored without encryption in ClipFoundry's database, which it makes readable only by your user account.
  • ClipFoundry's screens open in your web browser, and by default only your own computer can reach them (the address 127.0.0.1). If you choose to open them to your home network, anyone on that network can use them, so do that only on a network you trust. Connecting accounts, approving posts and publishing a clip yourself still work only from your own computer. But someone on your network could change settings (such as which AI service receives your transcripts), edit clip text, or turn Autopilot on. If automatic publishing for YouTube is on, Autopilot can then post to YouTube by itself.
  • Signing in uses the platforms' official sign-in pages, so your passwords never pass through ClipFoundry.

No software or computer is perfectly secure. Anyone who can use your Windows account can open ClipFoundry and see what it stores, so protect your computer with a password and keep it up to date.

7. Your choices

  • Disconnect an account: Settings → Accounts → Disconnect. ClipFoundry tries to cancel its access on the platform, then deletes the sign-in tokens and the account's ID, name and picture link on your computer. If you turned on automatic publishing, your channel's name stays in that permission's saved wording. It deletes them even if cancelling did not work, and it doesn't tell you whether cancelling worked, so to be sure, also remove access on the platform (sections 4 and 5). While an account needs to be reconnected, Disconnect is not shown; you can still remove access on the platform. Your post history stays until you delete the data folder. YouTube statistics are deleted as section 4 says. TikTok statistics stay until you delete the data folder.
  • Stop automatic publishing: turn off the automatic-publishing permission for YouTube in Autopilot. Posts it approved that are not uploaded yet go back to waiting for your OK. TikTok posts always wait for your OK.
  • Delete a video and its clips: in the Library. This deletes ClipFoundry's copy of the video, its clips, transcript and analysis. The original file in your own folders is not touched. Some records stay until you delete the data folder: the history of posts that were planned or published; for each clip Autopilot made, a short fingerprint (its title words, which video it came from, its start and end time, and short codes made from its words and pictures) that stops the same clip from being posted twice; and Autopilot's record of the video it came from (its title, link or file location, channel, and why it was used). Results and logs are kept as section 2 says.
  • Delete everything: first disconnect your accounts (Settings → Accounts → Disconnect), or remove ClipFoundry's access on the platform (sections 4 and 5). Then close ClipFoundry and delete its data folder. Deleting the folder alone does not cancel ClipFoundry's access to YouTube or TikTok. Your videos folder and any files you exported are separate, and stay until you delete them.
  • Stop watching a YouTube channel: remove it from the places Autopilot watches, and also remove its permission (Autopilot → Permissions & sources). While the permission stays, Autopilot still looks for that channel's new videos.
  • Optional services: turn off the free-license library or platform downloads, remove a Tavily key, or set AI scoring back to "Local" in Settings → Advanced.

8. Deleting on your computer doesn't delete on YouTube or TikTok

Once a video is posted, a copy belongs to your YouTube or TikTok account. Deleting the clip, disconnecting the account or deleting ClipFoundry does not remove that post. To remove it, delete it in YouTube Studio or in the TikTok app. YouTube and TikTok keep and handle what you posted under their own policies.

Autopilot uploads a planned YouTube post before its planned time (30 minutes before, unless you change this). If the post is public, YouTube makes it public at the planned time, even if your computer is off. Once it is uploaded, deleting the clip, turning off automatic publishing or disconnecting in ClipFoundry does not stop it. Change or delete it in YouTube Studio. A video sent to your TikTok inbox also stays there until you delete it in the TikTok app.
