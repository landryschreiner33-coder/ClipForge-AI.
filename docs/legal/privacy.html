Privacy Policy

ClipFoundry · Effective November 2, 2026

The short version

  • ClipFoundry is a desktop app that runs on your own computer. Your videos, clips, transcripts, settings and account connections are stored there. We don't run any server that receives them.
  • We, the publisher, receive nothing from the app: no videos, no account details, no usage statistics and no crash reports. The app has no ads, no tracking and no usage analytics.
  • Information leaves your computer only for features that need another service, for example posting to YouTube or TikTok, Autopilot's searches for videos, downloading the speech-recognition model or the picture, video and voice makers you set up, or an optional AI service you turn on. It goes straight from your computer to that service.
  • You sign in on Google's and TikTok's own pages. ClipFoundry never sees or stores your passwords.
  • If you enable the optional phone dashboard, a paired phone receives a small read-only status snapshot directly from your PC. It includes video titles, work progress, posting counts and system-check status. Account credentials, media, transcripts and Brain notes are excluded. We receive none of it.
  • Deleting a project removes its project media, clips and transcript, its ClipForge AI chat and the notes kept only for it. A separately submitted input copy, its source request and post history stay until you remove the data folder (section 7). Deleting does not remove a video you already posted on YouTube or TikTok. You do that on the platform.

1. Who this policy is from

ClipFoundry is published by Landry Schreiner ("we", "us"). You can reach us at landryschreiner456@gmail.com. This policy covers the ClipFoundry desktop app and this website.

The app runs on your computer and under your control, so you decide what it processes. We have no access to the information stored in it.

2. What ClipFoundry stores on your computer

ClipFoundry keeps its information in files in its data folder (the data folder inside the ClipFoundry folder, unless you chose another one). The default workflow accepts a link or file you submit; it does not search or admit watched-folder files automatically. Optional discovery mode can create/watch your videos folder (Videos\ClipFoundry). Existing folders are preserved when submitted mode is selected.

On macOS, the packaged app keeps its data in ~/Library/Application Support/ClipFoundry/data. Replacing the application does not delete that data. The native Mac window also keeps its interface preferences and cache in macOS-managed local WebKit storage, separately from your regular browser. Account sign-in opens in your default browser. These local stores are not sent to us.

ClipFoundry doesn't use cookies. The Follow system, Full or Reduced animation preference stays in your browser's local storage on this computer and is not sent to a platform. Clearing browser storage removes it; your operating system's motion preference still applies when Follow system is selected. This preference does not change video files or processing settings. The optional phone dashboard stores its access key in the phone browser's session storage by default; choosing Remember this phone stores it in local storage until you disconnect, clear browser storage or it expires. Access lasts up to 90 days. The PC stores only a SHA-256 hash of each device key, with creation and expiry times; expired records are removed during the next successful pairing. Unpair all phones deletes those records immediately. Pairing codes are single-use, expire after five minutes and are kept only in memory. Keys and codes are never placed in the dashboard address or sent to a publishing platform. When a screen shows your YouTube or TikTok profile picture, your browser loads that picture from Google's or TikTok's servers, which can see your computer's IP address.

Uploads and files added from this computer (added 2026-11-13). ClipForge keeps one copy of each file. For each upload, and each time you add a file from this computer, it keeps a record: the file's name, kind and size, how much of it arrived, whether it finished (or why not), the fingerprint your browser made of it (when it made one) and the name, size and change time your browser gave (so the same file can resume), when it started and finished, which stored file it became, and which project or example video took it. A file added from this computer is used where it is: ClipForge records where it is and a fingerprint of its contents, keeps its thumbnail and details in the data folder, and never changes or deletes your file. While a file is being sent, Stop, or removing the file from the page, only pauses the upload, and so does leaving or closing the page, a lost connection or an error while sending: the partly received file is kept so the upload can resume. Once it has had no progress for 3 days, it is deleted the next time you start an upload, and its record stays, marked as expired. Once every byte has arrived and ClipForge is checking the file, none of these stops it: the upload finishes and ClipForge keeps the file like any other finished upload, even if you removed it from the page. If an upload fails once all of it has arrived (for example, it does not match the fingerprint your browser made, or it is not a readable video, sound or picture file), what arrived is deleted at once and its record stays, marked as failed with the reason; uploading the file again starts a new upload. When you upload or add a file ClipForge already has, or its own work stores the same bytes again (an animated short's pictures and clips, the music and sound effects it adds to a short or to a video's edit, a link's download, or media added from a search, for a video or to your media only), it hands out the copy it has: it keeps the time of the last hand-out on that file's record, and notes whom each one went to (the project, or the upload or added file; for media added to your media only, the note names no one). Deleting a project keeps a copy handed out after its list was shown and within the hour before deleting when it went to an upload or added file no project or example video took yet, to another project that still exists, or to someone the note does not name; one handed out to the project itself, to a project deleted since, or as an upload a project or example video took, does not keep it (a file another project or example uses stays anyway; section 7). A note counts for an hour; it is removed at the next hand-out once it is older than that, or with its file. Upload records stay until ClipForge deletes its copy of their file (for a file added from this computer, its thumbnail and details; your own file stays): with the last project that used it, or with an example video when you also tick Also delete the video file (the file stays, and the example's delete says why, while a project or another example uses it, or while it was added again in the last day or handed out again within the hour and a page may be about to use it). If you run ClipForge in cloud mode on your own server, that data is stored on that server instead of your computer. Signing in uses your sign-in provider (for example Google): the server receives your account's stable ID, name and email address to recognise you, keeps only a fingerprint of your session cookie, and refuses every account that is not configured as the owner. Sessions end after 12 hours, after 4 idle hours, or when you sign out. Secrets are encrypted on the server with a key kept outside the data folder. Nothing is sent to any other new service.

4. YouTube API Services

ClipFoundry uses the YouTube API Services. By using its YouTube features you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and Google handles your information as the Google Privacy Policy describes.

When you connect YouTube, ClipFoundry asks Google for these permissions:

  • Manage your YouTube videos (youtube.upload). ClipFoundry uses it only to upload videos: the posts you approve, new qualifying clips covered by your input-scoped Make & upload request, and posts covered by optional standing automatic-publishing permission.
  • View your YouTube account (youtube.readonly), for your channel's name and picture, your channel's newest uploads, and the status and statistics of videos posted with ClipFoundry. If optional discovery is selected, Autopilot is on and you have not entered your own YouTube API key, it also uses this permission to find videos: it searches YouTube for your topics, reads YouTube's lists of popular and live videos, lists the new videos of your channel and of channels you added, gave a permission to or have an agreement with, reads the public details of the videos it finds, and checks which channel posted a video.
  • View YouTube Analytics reports (yt-analytics.readonly), for shares, watch time and how much of your posts people watched.

ClipFoundry uses this information on your computer: to publish, to show you status and results, to find videos for Autopilot, and to plan future posts. Some of it leaves your computer in two cases. If a clip comes from someone else's YouTube video under a Creative Commons license, its post on YouTube or TikTok includes a credit line with that video's title, channel name and link. If you turn on a cloud AI service, the YouTube title of a video Autopilot clips is sent to that service as the video's name (section 3). Nothing is sent to us.

ClipFoundry deletes YouTube information that is 30 days old: videos Autopilot found but did not use, the statistics of the videos it found, and the statistics of your own posts. It does this each time it starts and about once an hour while Autopilot is on. Your own posts' statistics are kept longer only if you turn on "Google approved derived metrics" in Settings because Google approved your project to keep them, and only while your channel stays connected. Some YouTube information is kept longer: your channel's name and picture link, until you disconnect; your channel's name in the saved wording of the automatic-publishing permission (if you ever turned it on), the title, channel and link of each video Autopilot worked on, and the ID and link of each of your posts, until you delete the data folder; and video titles in Autopilot's event history (90 days) and in the background log file (until you delete it).

You can remove ClipFoundry's access at any time in ClipFoundry (Settings → Accounts → Disconnect), or on Google's security settings page at https://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions. To delete what ClipFoundry stored on your computer, see section 7.

5. TikTok

When you connect TikTok, ClipFoundry asks TikTok for these permissions:

  • user.info.basic, for your display name and profile picture.
  • video.upload, to send a video to your TikTok inbox as a draft that you finish in the TikTok app (unless you turn this off).
  • video.publish, to post a video you approved straight to your profile (unless you turn this off).
  • video.list, for the statistics of your own videos (unless you turn this off).

The form helper lets you choose inbox, Direct Post only, or both, and optionally read your posted-video statistics. Apply the choices, save Settings and reconnect to request those permissions. Your existing connection keeps its previously granted scopes until reconnecting. A self-reported setup checklist cannot mark the app approved or audited. The helper checks public URL format without fetching the pages and cannot verify ownership, review or audit status. Downloading its draft form pack does not contact TikTok, submit a form or publish a website. Entered invalid public addresses, including credential-bearing or query-bearing links, block export; blank fields are allowed in an incomplete draft.

TikTok decides app eligibility and approvals. Its production guidelines reject personal/private utility tools, which can prevent this app from receiving posting access. Sandbox cannot post Public videos; unaudited Direct Post requires a private account and Only me. A private TikTok profile cannot offer Everyone. Every upload still needs your preview, supported privacy choice and per-post consent. If API access is unavailable, you can export the video and caption and post them yourself in TikTok.

ClipFoundry stores your sign-in tokens, your TikTok account ID, your display name, a link to your profile picture and the permissions you granted. For each post it stores the caption, the choices you made, the post's ID and status, and its statistics. For a post made straight to your profile, it also stores your TikTok user name. It reads the posting options TikTok offers for your account fresh each time, before you approve or post a video, and doesn't keep them. TikTok handles your information as TikTok's Privacy Policy describes. You can disconnect TikTok in ClipFoundry, or remove the app's access in the TikTok app's settings.

6. How your information is protected

  • On Windows, sign-in tokens, API keys and client secrets are encrypted with Windows' own data protection (DPAPI), so only your Windows account on that computer can read them. If Windows can't encrypt one, ClipFoundry stores it without encryption and notes that in its log. On macOS, newly saved secrets are encrypted with AES-GCM using a per-profile key in your login Keychain; saving fails if Keychain protection is unavailable. Older unencrypted Mac entries remain readable until saved again. Copying a Windows or Mac database alone does not transfer its protected sign-ins; reconnect those accounts on the new computer. In cloud mode (and on Linux when a key file is configured) secrets are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a separately stored key, and nothing is saved when the key is missing. Otherwise, on Linux, secrets are stored without encryption in ClipFoundry's database, which it makes readable only by your user account.
  • ClipFoundry's screens open in your web browser, and by default only your own computer can reach them (the address 127.0.0.1). If you choose to open them to your home network, anyone on that network can use them, so do that only on a network you trust. Connecting accounts, approving posts and publishing a clip yourself still work only from your own computer. But someone on your network could change settings (such as which AI service receives your transcripts), edit clip text, or turn Autopilot on. If automatic publishing for YouTube is on, Autopilot can then post to YouTube by itself.
  • Signing in uses the platforms' official sign-in pages, so your passwords never pass through ClipFoundry.
  • The phone dashboard is off by default and uses a separate private-network listener with device-key authorization. Only the desktop can enable it, generate pairing codes or unpair phones. The phone can read status but cannot change settings, connect accounts, approve posts, start jobs or access media. Tailscale encrypts the network connection; optional home Wi-Fi mode uses unencrypted HTTP, so use it only on a trusted network. Tailscale is a separate service you install and sign into yourself, under its own privacy policy. ClipFoundry does not install it, change a firewall or expose the main desktop listener for this feature.

No software or computer is perfectly secure. Anyone who can use your Windows account can open ClipFoundry and see what it stores, so protect your computer with a password and keep it up to date.

7. Your choices

  • Remove phone access: Settings → Integrations → Check from your phone → Unpair all phones deletes all paired-device grants. Turn off phone access stops the listener; grants remain until revoked or expired. Disconnect this phone clears that browser's key and displayed status. It does not revoke a copied key; unpair from the PC to revoke access. Clearing phone browser storage also removes its saved key.
  • Disconnect an account: Settings → Accounts → Disconnect. ClipFoundry tries to cancel its access on the platform, then deletes the sign-in tokens and the account's ID, name and picture link on your computer. If you turned on automatic publishing or made an input-scoped upload request, the recorded channel identity stays in its saved wording/history. It deletes them even if cancelling did not work, and it doesn't tell you whether cancelling worked, so to be sure, also remove access on the platform (sections 4 and 5). While an account needs to be reconnected, Disconnect is not shown; you can still remove access on the platform. Your post history stays until you delete the data folder. YouTube statistics are deleted as section 4 says. TikTok statistics stay until you delete the data folder.
  • Stop automatic publishing: Pause publishing holds new uploads globally. For a submitted input, use Missions → Submitted videos → Cancel video to stop its future work/uploads. Turning off separate standing permission affects its own unstarted approvals; it does not revoke a different input-scoped request. Completed and uncertain uploads retain their history/session. TikTok posts always require per-post consent.
  • Delete a project: in Projects (the trash button on its card), on a clips project's own page, or by asking ClipForge AI in a chat; a window (or ClipForge AI's card) first lists what goes and what stays, and the card's OK removes no file, copy of a video or planned post it did not list (what belongs to the project goes with it however much there is by then: its clips or versions, chat and notes, and what ClipForge makes for an animated short's scenes, also after the list was shown). This deletes the project's clips or a video's versions, transcript and analysis, its ClipForge AI chat and the notes kept only for it, and ClipFoundry's copy of the video when nothing else uses it; planned posts that have not started uploading are canceled. An animated short's recorded voices, and the originals the makers made for it with their notes, go with it, except an original kept with a picture or clip that stays (another project uses it, or it was added, uploaded or stored again for another page or project) and an original it could not take in, which stays in engines/made with its note. Each picture copied into ComfyUI's input folder to animate its scenes (a picture you chose included) stays there, and with the ComfyUI app or a ComfyUI at an address you typed, so do the copies that ComfyUI saved of what it made (section 2). Your own original is not touched, and a copy of a file you added again on another page since the day before the list was shown and have not used there yet, or one that was uploaded, added or stored again for another page or project after the list was shown and within the hour before deleting, stays (it may be about to be used there), unless it went to a project deleted since (also as an upload that project took); one that the project's own work stored again goes with it. Notes for all your videos stay. A separate uploaded source copy under submitted/, its request and source history remain until you delete the data folder. Some records stay until you delete the data folder: the history of posts that were planned or published; for each clip Autopilot made, a short fingerprint (its title words, which video it came from, its start and end time, and short codes made from its words and pictures) that stops the same clip from being posted twice; and Autopilot's record of the video it came from (its title, link or file location, channel, and why it was used). Results and logs are kept as section 2 says.
  • Delete everything: first disconnect your accounts (Settings → Accounts → Disconnect), or remove ClipFoundry's access on the platform (sections 4 and 5). Then close ClipFoundry and delete its data folder. Deleting the folder alone does not cancel ClipFoundry's access to YouTube or TikTok. Your videos folder and any files you exported are separate, and stay until you delete them, and so do the models ClipForge put in the ComfyUI app's own folders and what that app kept (remove those in the app).
  • Remove the picture, video and voice makers: in Settings → Pictures, video, voice. Remove ComfyUI deletes ClipForge's copy of ComfyUI and its Python environment; the models, the private Python, uv, the work folders, the logs and the made files stay. A model's Remove deletes the files ClipForge downloaded for it into the ComfyUI in use (a part another downloaded model still needs stays), and the voice's Remove deletes its folder. An unfinished ComfyUI set-up or voice download has no Remove; its partly downloaded files stay until it finishes or you delete the data folder. Files in engines/made, voice samples and pictures in ClipForge's own ComfyUI's input folder have no delete button; they go with the data folder. With the ComfyUI app (or a ComfyUI address you typed), the pictures it was given to animate and the pictures and clips it saved stay in that ComfyUI's folders until you delete them there.
  • Stop watching a YouTube channel: remove it from the places Autopilot watches, and also remove its permission (Autopilot → Permissions & sources). While optional discovery is selected and the permission stays, Autopilot can still look for that channel's new videos. Submitted mode admits no new channel videos.
  • Optional services: turn off the free-license library or platform downloads, remove a Tavily key, or set AI scoring back to "Local" in Settings → Advanced. Settings → Integrations → NVIDIA AI → Disconnect removes the NVIDIA key and your agreement and turns NVIDIA AI off.

8. Deleting on your computer doesn't delete on YouTube or TikTok

Once a video is posted, a copy belongs to your YouTube or TikTok account. Deleting the clip, disconnecting the account or deleting ClipFoundry does not remove that post. To remove it, delete it in YouTube Studio or in the TikTok app. YouTube and TikTok keep and handle what you posted under their own policies.

New planned Public YouTube posts upload at their planned local time only after explicit audience confirmation and suitable account-bound approval, an input-scoped request or optional standing automatic-publishing permission. Existing scheduled Private posts stay Private, may upload ahead of their planned time, and still need your Studio invitations for selected viewers. ClipFoundry does not use YouTube’s publishAt field. It reads back actual visibility and reports restrictions. Once uploaded, deleting the clip, stopping automation or disconnecting does not remove the video; change or delete it in YouTube Studio. A TikTok inbox draft stays there until you delete it in TikTok. Older public plans held by the previous migration remain held; the existing backup remains in data\backups.
